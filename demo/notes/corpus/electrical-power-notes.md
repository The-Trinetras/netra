# Electrical power: study notes

Power (P, in watts) is the rate at which a component turns electrical energy into
heat, light or motion.

## Formulas

- P = V x I (voltage times current).
- Using Ohm's law to substitute for V: P = I x I x R, written P = I^2 x R.

## Worked examples

- A lamp runs at 12 V and draws 0.5 A. Its power is 12 x 0.5 = 6 W.
- A 5 ohm resistor carries a current of 2 A. Its power is
  P = I^2 x R = 2 x 2 x 5 = 20 W.

## Note

Power is measured in watts. One watt is one joule of energy per second.
