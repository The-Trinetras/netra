# Ohm's law: study notes

Voltage (V, in volts) pushes current (I, in amperes) through a resistor. The
resistor's resistance (R, in ohms) says how hard it pushes back.

Ohm's law states that V = I x R. Rearranged, R = V / I and I = V / R.

## Worked table: one resistor

We measured the same resistor at three settings of the power supply.

| Voltage (V) | Current (A) | V / I (ohms) |
|-------------|-------------|--------------|
| 2           | 1           | 2            |
| 4           | 2           | 2            |
| 6           | 3           | 2            |

Every row gives V / I = 2 ohms. The resistance stays the same while the voltage
changes, so the graph of voltage against current is a straight line through the
origin. A resistor that behaves this way is called ohmic.

## Common mistake

Students often say that doubling the voltage doubles the resistance. It does not.
Doubling the voltage doubles the current, and the resistance stays at 2 ohms.
